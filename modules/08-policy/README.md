# Module 8. Integrated Regional Application and Policy Evaluation

Draft teaching plan for instructor review.

When does a modeled increase in regional activity support a policy recommendation? Students compare a policy with an explicit counterfactual and identify whose gains, costs, and external effects enter the evaluation (LO7, drawing on LO6).

## Progression of ideas

1. **Frame a decision.** Specify the intervention, its alternative, the regional boundary, and whose well-being matters. Use Module 3’s measurement choices to decide which outcomes can be compared.
2. **Match each claim to evidence.** Use Modules 6 and 7 to explain changes in production and income under each scenario. Draw on Modules 4 and 5 when a change in rents or amenities matters to residents.
3. **Reach a conditional policy judgment.** Weigh benefits against displaced activity, resource costs, and external effects. Identify who gains or loses and which missing evidence could reverse the conclusion. The resulting tool is a comparison students can defend even when the models supply only some of the quantities they need.

## Topic outline

### The policy choice and its alternative

- Define the intervention, decision maker, region, affected residents, and feasible counterfactual using the PS 4 scenario.
- Identify displaced spending and alternative uses of labor and capital. Decide which changes count as opportunity costs.

**Instructor notes:**

### Matching evidence to policy claims

- Compare IO and CGE predictions for a common shock. Identify which quantities each model measures and the assumptions behind their differences.
- Consider workers, renters, and landowners separately. Draw on the spatial equilibrium and hedonic material to interpret changes in rents or amenities.

**Instructor notes:**

### A conditional policy recommendation

- Assess external effects and distributional consequences alongside production and income. Check for overlap before combining benefits.
- Explain which alternative the evidence favors, who gains or loses, and what missing evidence or assumption could reverse the judgment.
