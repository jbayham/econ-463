# Module 3. Defining and Measuring a Regional Economy

Draft teaching plan for instructor review.

Which regional boundary and benchmark make a claim about specialization meaningful? In class, students build a regional profile in R and explain how the choice of comparison affects it (LO5, building on LO3).

## Progression of ideas

1. **Choose the economy to measure.** Modules 1 and 2 explain why firms cluster and how growth crosses boundaries. Use the commuting corridor to decide which workers and firms belong in a regional comparison.
2. **Separate specialization from size.** Compare a sector’s share of regional employment with its share in a benchmark economy. This gives the location quotient an economic interpretation before students calculate it in R.
3. **Move from levels to change.** Ask how much employment would have grown if local industries had followed benchmark trends. Use shift-share to build a regional profile and identify a pattern that calls for an explanation. Module 4 supplies one possible mechanism through household and firm location choices.

## Topic outline

### Regional boundaries and comparable data

- Administrative regions and functional labor markets. The Module 2 commuting corridor illustrates how residence and workplace counts can describe different economies.
- Comparability across sector definitions, geographic boundaries, units, and years. Which employment figures can support a common comparison?

**Instructor notes:**

### Specialization relative to a benchmark

- Employment levels, sector shares, and the choice of reference economy. A small region can specialize in an industry that employs more people elsewhere.
- Location quotients by hand and in R. Consider how changing the benchmark changes the regional profile.

**Instructor notes:**

### Sources of regional employment change

- Shift-share decomposition into overall benchmark growth, industry mix, and regional shift.
- Interpret the regional shift as a departure from expected growth. Identify a location mechanism and the evidence needed to investigate it.

## Readings and teaching materials

Schaffer, ch. 2, pp. 6–9, motivates boundary choice. Note A, pp. 43–47, supplies the measurement techniques. For a second treatment of functional regions and specialization, use Hoover and Giarratani, §§9.1–9.4 and appendix 12-1.

## Assessment connection

On PS 1, students use the supplied job counts to calculate location quotients and shift-share components and show their work on the handout. Exam 1 can ask students to interpret a quotient or a growth component.
