# Module 7. Regional CGE Modeling and Model Comparison

Draft teaching plan for instructor review.

How does the impact estimate change when firms and households respond to prices and resources are scarce? Students reproduce a benchmark with a supplied computable general equilibrium model in R, run a counterfactual, and explain the difference from IO (LO6).

## Teaching sequence

- **Dec 10–11.** Move from IO flows to a social accounting matrix. Trace payments from firms to factors to households and back to producers. Connect the flows to production choices, household budgets, and market clearing. Introduce PS 4 on Dec 11.
- **Dec 14.** Identify the price numeraire and closure assumptions. Use the supplied model to reproduce benchmark quantities and prices and check market balances before applying a shock.
- **Dec 15.** Apply the agreed shock and compare results with the IO scenario. Trace a change in factor demand through wages, substitution, and regional trade.
- **Dec 16, before M8.** Vary one assumption, such as labor mobility, and explain which result changes and why. Carry the comparison into the policy discussion.

## Readings and teaching materials

Hewings, §7.2, pp. 52–54, and §7.4, pp. 55–57, bridges IO and social accounting. The workshop SAM elements description supports account mapping. A model-specific guide still needs to cover calibration, closure, solution in R, and the interpretation of outputs.

## Assessment connection

PS 4 checkpoint on Dec 15 covers the baseline, scenario output, and mechanism. Sensitivity and interpretation complete the submission on Dec 16.
